# Quadrature

A quadrature rule is data: nodes $x_n$ and weights $w_n$ with $\int g \mathrm{d}x \approx \sum_n w_n g(x_n)$. `Rule` holds the two arrays and nothing else; integrix sums, scans and adapts with it (integrix design decision 3), and unpacking a rule gives `(nodes, weights)`.

Four constructors cover the 1-D rules. `gauss_legendre(n, interval)` is the $n$-point Gauss–Legendre rule, exact for polynomials of degree up to $2n - 1$, and `clenshaw_curtis(n, interval)` the $n$-point Clenshaw–Curtis rule on the Chebyshev extreme points, exact to degree $n - 1$ ($n$ when $n - 1$ is even). Both are built on the host in float64, so they can size static arrays, and map affinely from $[-1, 1]$ onto any interval. `trapezoid(nodes)` and `simpson(nodes)` take the nodes a field already lives on, uniform or not, ascending or descending; they equal `scipy.integrate.trapezoid` and `scipy.integrate.simpson` (with its correction for an even node count) and are traceable, so their weights are differentiable in the nodes. The sphere grids in `functax.geometry` carry their own weights.

## Rules on the sphere

`SphereRule` is a `Rule` whose nodes are unit vectors $\vect{u}_q$, shape $(Q, 3)$, with weights for $\int_{S^2} g \mathrm{d}A \approx \sum_q w_q g(\vect{u}_q)$ on a sphere of radius $a$ (keyword `radius`, 1 by default), so the weights sum to $4 \pi a^2$. Its `degree` is the highest $L$ for which every spherical harmonic $Y_l^m$ with $l \le L$ is integrated exactly. `sphere_gauss_legendre(n_lat, n_lon)` takes the sines of latitude at the Gauss–Legendre nodes (ascending, south to north) and $n_{\text{lon}}$ uniform longitudes from $-\pi$, and is exact to degree $\min(2 n_{\text{lat}} - 1, n_{\text{lon}} - 1)$; spectraldiffx orders the same nodes from north to south and its longitudes from 0. `sphere_latlon(n_lat, n_lon)` weights equiangular cells by their exact areas and is exact to degree 1 only. `sphere_healpix(nside)` and `sphere_fibonacci(n)` give every node the equal area $4 \pi a^2 / Q$; they are not exact beyond degree 1 (HEALPix, by its symmetry) and 0 (Fibonacci). Each wraps the matching grid in `functax.geometry`.

## Cell areas on grids

`cell_area(grid)` is the rule of a grid's cells: its nodes are the cell centres and its weights the cell areas $\lvert C_k \rvert$, one per cell in the order of the field's flattened values, so a field of cell means integrates as $\sum_k \lvert C_k \rvert y_k$. Every pixel of an `AffineGrid` $(a, b, c, d, e, f)$ has area $\lvert ae - bd \rvert$; a `RectilinearGrid` takes the product of each axis's cell widths from `Axis.cell_edges` (the axis's `edges`, or the midpoints between nodes). With `sphere_radius=a` and `unit=` the coordinates are longitude and latitude, and a cell has area $a^2 \lvert \Delta \lambda \rvert \lvert \sin \varphi_{\text{top}} - \sin \varphi_{\text{bottom}} \rvert$, so a global grid sums to $4 \pi a^2$. On the sphere an `AffineGrid` must be north-up (rows are latitude) and a `RectilinearGrid` has two axes in $(\lambda, \varphi)$ order, as `Conservative(sphere=True, unit=…)` reads them. The radius is the caller's; functax holds no Earth constants.

## Rules on curves

`polyline(vertices, n, rule=…)` is a rule along a polyline with vertices $\vect{v}_0, \dots, \vect{v}_{V-1}$: each segment gets $n$ nodes from the midpoint rule (`rule="midpoint"`, the default) or the Gauss–Legendre rule (`rule="gauss_legendre"`, exact for polynomials along the segment up to degree $2n - 1$), with arc-length weights $\Delta s_k$ that sum to the polyline's length. It returns a `CurveRule`, a `Rule` with one more field, `normals`: in 2-D the unit tangent of each node's segment rotated by $+90°$, so a flux across a transect is $\Phi = \sum_k \Delta s_k c(\vect{x}_k) (\vect{u}_k \cdot \vect{n}_k)$ and reversing the vertices flips its sign; in any other dimension a curve has no single normal and `normals` is `None`. The node count $K = n (V - 1)$ is static, so the rule traces under `jit` and `vmap` and differentiates in the vertices.
